# Working on your manuscript with scholium

Keep writing in Word. Use Codex to explore your chapters, test an interpretation, or see how a revision affects the rest of your book. Scholium helps Codex find the passages and notes on which its answers depend.

If you are already using your book in Codex, you can start with any question below. For a new book or computer, use the separate [setup guide](CODEX_WORKFLOW.md).

## Find the passage you remember

You do not need to remember the chapter or exact wording. Give a name, phrase or description:

> Where do I discuss Hirschfeld’s relationship to the early rights organisations?

> Find passages where I distinguish individual identity from a sense of collective belonging.

> Show me the endnote supporting that statement, and the paragraph around it.

Describe the idea in more than one way if the first search is unhelpful. A search that finds nothing does not prove the idea is absent from your book.

## Compare arguments across chapters

Ask a question that matters to your argument:

> How does Goethe’s role in the introduction differ from his role in the chapter on aestheticist narcissism?

> Does the conclusion deliver what the introduction promises?

> Compare my accounts of belonging in these two chapters. Are they compatible, or do I need to explain a difference?

Ask Codex to show the evidence for a comparison. A useful answer distinguishes what your manuscript says from the interpretation being offered. Two passages can differ in period, subject or scope without contradicting each other.

## Review terminology, people and chronology

> Am I using “homosexual”, “gay” and “queer” consistently? Show where their meanings or purposes differ.

> Do these references to Müller concern the same person?

> Compare the dates and boundaries of the period I give in the introduction and conclusion. Flag anything that needs clarification.

You can ask for English and German terms to be considered together. Namesakes, translated terms and changes in historical usage need interpretation; a matching word is only a starting point.

## Make a chapter summary or argument map

> Read chapter 3 and its notes. Save a concise summary of its central argument, supporting claims and unresolved questions.

> Map the relationship between identity, belonging and imagined community in this chapter. Give a passage for each connection.

> Show my saved chapter summaries and tell me which need updating.

Saved summaries remain attached to the chapter versions they describe. When a chapter changes, scholium identifies affected summaries as outdated. Ask Codex to read the revised chapter and save a fresh account; the earlier one remains available.

These are aids to your reading, not replacements for the manuscript. A summary of one chapter should not be presented as a judgment on the whole book.

## Follow the consequences of a revision

Save your changes in Word, then ask:

> Refresh my book and compare the latest version of this chapter with the previous one. What changed in the argument?

> Which passages elsewhere might need reconsideration because of this change? Explain the connection before suggesting an edit.

> Have I changed a claim that the conclusion still relies on?

Codex can compare saved versions and investigate related passages. Its suggestions should tell you what may be affected and why. They are possibilities for review, not automatic findings of contradiction. Only versions previously recorded by scholium can be compared.

## Decide what to do with suggestions

You remain the editor. Ask for more context, reject an interpretation, or record a decision:

> Explain the evidence behind this suggestion.

> Dismiss this suggestion: the two passages concern different periods.

> Accept this as something I need to revise, and keep the other suggestions open.

Accepting a suggestion records your decision. It does not change your manuscript. Make the actual revision in Word, then ask Codex to read the latest saved chapters.

## Use it during an ordinary writing day

1. Write and save in Word as usual.
2. Ask Codex to refresh your book and work from the latest saved chapters. If automatic updating is already running, Codex still checks that it has the current material.
3. Ask your scholarly question. Follow up with “Show me the passage”, “Read the note”, or “Compare that with the conclusion”.
4. Save useful summaries or review decisions, and make your own revisions in Word.

You do not need to open the scholium application’s source folder or type search commands to do this. Work in the book folder you connected to Codex.

## Know what an answer can establish

Expect references that let you return to the relevant chapter, passage or note, and an explanation when the evidence is incomplete. Crossed-out text should be identified as such. Ask to see the source whenever a conclusion seems stronger than its support.

Scholium can work with ordinary chapter text, footnotes, endnotes, German and English text, and caption text. It does not interpret the contents of images, supply dependable Word page numbers, or independently check the publications mentioned in your notes. Unresolved tracked changes and some unusual Word features can prevent a chapter from being read fully; Codex should tell you when that affects an answer.

If a chapter is missing or has changed since it was last read, earlier material may still be available. It must be identified as earlier material, not quietly presented as your current text.

## Protect your work

Continue your normal backups of the Word documents. You can also ask:

> Back up scholium’s saved readings, chapter history, summaries and review decisions, and verify the backup.

That backup protects scholium’s records; it does not replace your Word-document backups. If something goes wrong, ask Codex to recover a verified backup into a separate folder and explain what it recovered before switching to it. The recovery process preserves the existing working copy.

Your manuscript is kept in your chosen folders. Passages read in a Codex conversation are shared with the Codex service for that conversation. Scholium does not send chapters to a background AI service or automatically edit your Word files.

## If something seems wrong

Tell Codex what you expected and what happened:

> I saved this chapter, but your answer still uses the old wording. Check that you are reading the latest version.

> You say these claims conflict. Show both passages with their surrounding paragraphs and notes.

> This chapter is missing from your answer. Check whether you could read it.

A failed reading of one chapter should not silently erase earlier work or make the rest of the book disappear. For installation or recovery assistance, the separate [maintenance guide](OPERATIONS.md) has the technical details.
